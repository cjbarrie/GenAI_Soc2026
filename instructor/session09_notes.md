# Instructor notes — Session 9: LLMs at work

## Central question

When an LLM helps someone do their job, what changes: performance, knowledge or organizational position?

## Argument

Marx and Braverman place technology inside relations of production and managerial control over knowledge. Burawoy adds worker practice and consent inside the labour process. Barley shows that the same technology can become part of different occupational settlements. Zuboff adds the informational record produced by computer-mediated work.

Do not collapse the two contemporary findings. Brynjolfsson et al. estimate changes in workplace performance while assistance is available. Wiles et al. use a separate experiment to test retained knowledge without assistance. Their comparison shows why outcomes must be distinguished; it does not establish whether the customer-support workers learned.

## Coding emphasis

The function is new; the underlying operations are not. Replace both parameters with their literal values before reading the function body. Keep both route branches visible. After the first return, slow down at `worker_next_step`: the exercise should feel like a worker deciding whether to ask again, escalate or begin writing. The second call occurs only on the supplied `ask_model_again` path. The software checks target the baseline comparison, causal claim and decision status. They do not validate the sociological argument.

After the second reply, ask students to identify which reasoning the assistant supplied, which choice the consultant retained and what the saved record makes visible to the organization. Do not treat a recorded final action as evidence that the worker possessed meaningful organizational authority.
