# Week 11 — AI-assisted writing and social class

**Question:** Does class background change how AI use is judged?

**Essential readings (complete articles):**

- Martha Foschi (2000), “Double Standards for Competence: Theory and Research,” *Annual Review of Sociology* 26: 21–42.
- Jessica A. Reif, Richard P. Larrick and Jack B. Soll (2025), “Evidence of a Social Evaluation Penalty for Using Artificial Intelligence,” *PNAS* 122: e2426766122.
- Matthew H. Rafalow (2018), “Disciplining Play: Digital Youth Culture as Capital at School,” *AJS* 123(5): 1416–1452.
- Oliver Schilke and Martin Reimann (2025), “The Transparency Dilemma: How AI Disclosure Erodes Trust,” *OBHDP* 188: 104405.

The opening lecture uses a fictional competitor report and two family-background descriptions to ask how readers judge its author. The middle of class discusses Bourdieu’s linguistic market, competence standards, AI-use stigma, institutional valuation of digital practices and disclosure. The final Python walkthrough edits the report and prepares four reader descriptions crossing family background and AI disclosure. It does not collect human judgments. The descriptions require pretesting.
