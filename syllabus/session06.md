# Session 6 — What changes when a model starts asking the questions?

**Date:** Wednesday, October 7, 2026 · 9:30 a.m.–12:15 p.m.  
**Methodological domain:** AI-assisted and AI-led interviewing

## Substantive question

What kind of evidence does an interview produce, what may the interviewer do, and what changes when a model can choose or write the next probe?

## Learning objectives

Students should be able to distinguish interview purposes; assess probes for specificity, direction and comparability; explain how the relationship and mode help produce an account; distinguish a protocol from the path actually received; distinguish STT, a text interviewer and TTS; and explain how a participant answer is added to the conversation before the model generates the next probe.

## Required readings

- Merton and Kendall (1946), [“The Focused Interview”](https://doi.org/10.1086/219886), complete article.
- Geiecke and Jaravel (2026), [“The Rise of AI-Led Interviews in Social Science”](https://benjamin.geiecke.com/files/ai_interviews.pdf), complete paper.
- Rivera (2012), [“Hiring as Cultural Matching”](https://doi.org/10.1177/0003122412463213), complete article.

## Lecture argument

Rivera's study shows how interviews and observation can uncover the criteria evaluators actually use—in this case, cultural matching in elite hiring—and why a generic question is not enough. Merton and Kendall help us evaluate the probes that produce this kind of evidence. Geiecke and Jaravel then show what changes when software can select or compose those probes during the interview.

## Computational trace and completion task

Students pass a participant answer and interviewing instruction to a hosted or local model, retrieve one adaptive probe and append it to the conversation record. They change the answer and repeat the call. The completion submission is a narrated screen recording explaining the messages, call, return and appended record, plus one reading-based limit.
