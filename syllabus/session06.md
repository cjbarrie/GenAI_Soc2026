# Session 6 — What changes when a model starts asking the questions?

**Date:** Wednesday, October 7, 2026 · 9:30 a.m.–12:15 p.m.  
**Methodological domain:** AI-assisted and AI-led interviewing

## Substantive question

What kind of evidence does an interview produce, what may the interviewer do, and what changes when a model can choose or write the next probe?

## Learning objectives

Students should be able to distinguish interview purposes; assess probes for specificity, direction and comparability; explain how the relationship and mode help produce an account; distinguish a protocol from the path actually received; distinguish STT, a text interviewer and TTS; and explain every line and limit of `audit_interview`.

## Required readings

- Merton and Kendall (1946), [“The Focused Interview”](https://doi.org/10.1086/219886), selected sections.
- Geiecke and Jaravel (2026), [“The Rise of AI-Led Interviews in Social Science”](https://benjamin.geiecke.com/files/ai_interviews.pdf), selected sections.
- Jack, Cooper and Flower (2026), [“Automating the Qualitative Interview? Using Gen AI Chatbots in Social Science Research”](https://doi.org/10.1177/20597991261448157).

## Lecture argument

LLMs redistribute decisions sociologists have long debated: when to probe, how to clarify, how much to standardize and when to stop. Assignment to the same interview system therefore does not guarantee exposure to the same interview.

## Computational trace and completion task

Students pass a participant answer and interviewing instruction to a hosted or local model, retrieve one adaptive probe and append it to the conversation record. They change the answer and repeat the call. The completion submission is a narrated screen recording explaining the messages, call, return and appended record, plus one reading-based limit. Upload to the Box folder supplied by the instructor.
