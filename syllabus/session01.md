## Substantive question

What kind of research technology is an LLM?

## Learning objectives

By the end, students should be able to:

1. state the inferential target: describe one model-assisted operation without mistaking its output for evidence.
2. explain the reading disagreement in their own words;
3. identify the Python type of every major input and output in the worked trace;
4. explain each line or small block of `build_research_record`;
5. use the validation diagnostic to bound a sociological claim.

## Required readings

- [Davidson & Karell (2025), integrating GenAI into social science](https://doi.org/10.1177/00491241251339184)
- [Alvero et al. (2026), GenAI in sociological research](https://doi.org/10.15195/v13.a3)

## Lecture argument

An LLM becomes a research technology only when its input, transformation, output, and validation record are visible. Capability maps show where models enter research; disciplinary evidence asks what becomes easier, what becomes hidden, and who bears the checking work.

## Computational trace

Students move from visible synthetic inputs through `build_research_record` to a printed output and explicit checks. The required task uses no live API. Every code block is read as **input → Python type → operation → output → research meaning**.

## Weekly completion task

Build one complete research record from a prompt, settings, and cached response. Submit a prediction, working code, interpreted check, and 100–150 word reading-linked claim. Completion marks only; missing elements may be supplied within one week.

## Validation focus

A persuasive answer is copied into an analysis while the model, settings, prompt, raw response, and validation decision remain unknown.
