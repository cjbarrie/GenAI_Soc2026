# Week 11 literature audit — AI use and judgments of competence

## Question and limits

The lecture asks how AI assistance changes judgments of a worker, and whether the worker's status affects those judgments. The established findings below do **not** show that lower-status AI users face a larger penalty. That interaction is the proposed study's question. Organizational rank is also not a direct measure of social class.

## Core papers

| Paper | Evidence and result | Teaching job | Limit for our question |
|---|---|---|---|
| Foschi (2000), *Annual Review of Sociology* 26: 21–42, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.soc.26.1.21) | Review of experimental research on status-based standards for competence, including reverse double standards | Explain why identical work need not carry identical evidence of ability | No AI use and no direct estimate of a workplace AI penalty |
| Reif, Larrick and Soll (2025), *PNAS* 122: e2426766122, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2426766122) | Four preregistered experiments. Study 2 varies AI, human or no help in occupational vignettes; mean laziness ratings 2.50, 2.16 and 2.02. No significant moderation by worker age, gender or occupation in that study | Direct evidence about observers' evaluation of AI users | Occupation is not our within-organization senior/junior manipulation; no class effect identified |
| Rafalow (2018), *AJS* 123(5): 1416–1452, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1086/695766) | Comparative ethnography of three schools; teachers interpreted students' digital expression differently across school settings | Show institutional valuation of digital practice before LLMs | Class, race and school context covary; no AI manipulation |
| Schilke and Reimann (2025), *OBHDP* 188: 104405, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.obhdp.2025.104405) | Preregistered experiments on AI disclosure and trust. Study 11 varies prior knowledge and report disclosure; mean trust 4.10 with disclosure and 4.62 without, d=.50 | Show a different outcome and a report task close to our running example | Rank is held fixed; disclosure is not the same as demonstrated incompetence |

## Supporting theory

Bourdieu (1977), “The Economics of Linguistic Exchanges,” defines the problem of language's value in a social market. Lamont and Lareau (1988), “Cultural Capital,” press us to identify the particular signals institutions recognize. Neither is evidence of an AI-specific penalty.

## Moved out of the main deck

Alvero et al. (2021), Huang (2024), and Lee et al. (2026) remain optional admissions readings. Their methods and findings are valuable, but the admissions-essay case no longer organizes the lecture. Kadoma, Metaxa and Naaman (2025) is also a possible later extension on suspected AI authorship; its mixed demographic findings should not be presented as a general class or nationality gradient.
