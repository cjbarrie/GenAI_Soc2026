# Week 11 teaching architecture

**Question:** Does class background change how AI use is judged?

**Bounded conclusion:** Experiments identify penalties in how AI users are judged for effort, ability and trust. Bourdieu, Foschi and Rafalow show why the evaluator and setting matter. None of the assigned experiments tests whether the penalty differs by family background. Either direction is possible.

## Lecture, reading discussion and coding

| Time | Material | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 0–10 min | Identical fictional competitor report; two family-background descriptions | Introduce the class question while keeping job and report fixed |
| 10–22 min | Bourdieu, then Reif Study 2 | Establish the social setting of evaluation, then the AI-use penalty |
| 22–32 min | Foschi | Explain status-based competence thresholds and the reverse prediction |
| 32–39 min | Lamont and Lareau | Specify which signs of competence the workplace recognizes |
| 39–47 min | Rafalow | Show a pre-LLM case of institutions recognizing similar digital practices differently |
| 47–57 min | Smart Compose comparison; Schilke and Reimann Study 11 | Distinguish routine writing assistance, disclosure and trust |
| 57–65 min | Two competing class predictions and proposed 2×2 | State what new human-reader evidence and pretests would be needed |
| 65–120 min | Discussion of the four required papers | Compare designs, outcomes, status measures and alternative predictions |
| 120–165 min | Beginner Python walkthrough | Edit one report, inspect raw return, fact-check it, and construct four identical-text reader versions. No video is due and no work is assigned for the cancelled class. |

The main example is instructor-authored. The LLM's live revision is model output; the four descriptions are proposed experimental materials; no human outcomes are observed. The same distinction is maintained in the slides, book and notebook.
