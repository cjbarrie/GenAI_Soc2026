# Midterm project proposal

**Release:** after Session 6, October 7.  
**Due:** before Session 7, October 21.  
**Length:** 1,200–1,500 words, plus references and a short appendix if needed.
**Weight:** 20%, followed by a separate 5% oral walkthrough.

## Purpose

The proposal is the first substantial version of your final project. It should identify a sociologically important question, explain what evidence would answer it, and show where an LLM would—and would not—enter the research design. You are not expected to have completed the study or reviewed every relevant publication.

## What to submit

Submit one proposal containing:

1. **Research question and motivation:** the question, why it matters sociologically, and the claim the project would eventually seek to support.
2. **Position in the literature:** a focused account of the small body of work needed to locate the project and identify the gap or disagreement it addresses.
3. **Evidence and research design:** proposed data, cases or participants; sampling or case-selection logic; unit of analysis; and the comparison or evidence needed for the claim.
4. **Role of the LLM:** the specific operation the model would perform and why it is useful here. Distinguish the model's role from the researcher's decisions.
5. **Worked example:** one concrete input → model operation → expected output → researcher check. This may use cached, synthetic or illustrative material and need not be a complete working system.
6. **Validation, failure and ethics:** what could go wrong, how those failures would be detected, and any issues involving privacy, consent, representation, access or data ownership.
7. **Reproducibility plan:** what would be saved so that another researcher could understand and, where possible, repeat the analysis.
8. **Development plan:** the next two or three steps required to turn the proposal into the final research-design package.

The proposal is a plan for a feasible project, not a promise to carry out a complete LLM-assisted study during the semester.

## AI use

The course policy applies: assistance is permitted with disclosure, but you remain responsible for every claim, citation and piece of code or model output included. Add a short disclosure explaining what assistance you used and how you checked it. The oral walkthrough is separate and takes place without live AI.

## Marking dimensions

- sociological importance and a clearly stated research question;
- fit between the proposed claim, evidence and research design;
- a specific and justified role for the LLM;
- a concrete, intelligible worked example;
- credible validation, failure analysis and ethical safeguards;
- focused use of relevant literature;
- a feasible development plan, transparent record and responsible disclosure.
