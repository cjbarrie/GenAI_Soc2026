# Session 10 reading guide — Writing, influence and relationships

## The distinction to carry into class

A generated message, an effect on its writer and a consequence for a relationship are three different outcomes. The required readings help define and observe them without collapsing one into another.

| Reading | Main object | Question for discussion |
|---|---|---|
| Zelizer (2012) | How people match ties, transactions, media and meanings | How might the relationship change the meaning of an AI-assisted condolence? |
| Hohenstein et al. (2023) | AI suggestions inside a conversation | Which results concern actual use, suspected use, language and partner judgment? |
| Williams-Ceci et al. (2026) | Influence on the writer during composition | Why is a shift in the writer's reported attitude not evidence of recipient persuasion? |

## Before class

Bring one passage from Zelizer that helps explain why the same transfer can mean different things in different relationships, one feature of the Hohenstein design that supports a causal claim, and one limitation on the Williams-Ceci conclusion. Be ready to say what evidence you would need to determine whether an AI-assisted condolence helped or harmed a relationship.

## Reading to code

The workbook asks the model to advise on a condolence, a workplace-credit dispute and a request to protect private family information. Students record their own response before the model call and compare it with the suggestion afterwards. The exercise does not manufacture a score for sincerity or relationship quality. Use the readings to identify what is visible in the record and what would require evidence from senders, recipients or an interaction over time.
