# Session 6 reading guide — AI-assisted and AI-led interviewing

## One question links the readings

What is the interview meant to produce, and what does the interviewer have to do for that evidence to be useful?

| Reading | Read for | Bring to class |
|---|---|---|
| [Merton & Kendall (1946)](https://doi.org/10.1086/219886) | specificity, range, depth and non-direction | one useful probe and one risk created by probing |
| [Geiecke & Jaravel (2026)](https://benjamin.geiecke.com/files/ai_interviews.pdf) | how protocol, history and model produce the next question | one strength and one claim the evaluation does not settle |
| [Jack, Cooper & Flower (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1177/20597991261448157) | repetition, nuance and participant experience | one point where completion and quality diverge |

## Before class

Identify one claim an interview should support, one interviewer action that helps, one action that might direct the answer, and one fact about the realized interview that should be stored.

## Reading → code

`audit_interview` reconstructs protocol questions and adaptive probes in order. Decide what this record can establish and which judgments—depth, rapport, validity and participant understanding—still require other evidence.
